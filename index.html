Case studies

Shamrock Foods · Operations Analytics Intern · Phoenix · 2026

How many people does tomorrow's outbound shift need?

Warehouse staffing was planned without a forecast. I built one that predicts daily labor demand and put it in a tool that planners can open themselves.

01Raw records3M+ warehouse records, 12+ months
02Clean and aggregatePySpark and SQL in Databricks, down to daily demand
03ModelTree-based models, compared on held-out data
04ForecastDaily demand plus a 90-day outlook
05Hand overApp and dashboard for the planning team

What I decided

  • Forecast by job type (pickers, loaders, captains) and by temperature zone (dry, chill, freezer). One total number does not help a planner who cannot move a freezer picker to the dry dock.
  • Ship it as an app and a dashboard on top of saved tables. A notebook would have stopped being used the day I left.
  • Add a what-if view for cross-training after the operations lead asked what would change if workers could cover more than one role.

What was hard

  • The model took seven versions. Early ones looked fine on average and missed badly on specific zones.
  • There was no data on which workers have which skills, so the cross-training scenario had to be built as an explicit assumption that the user can change.
4.58%MAPE on held-out test data
$1.8Mprojected annual savings

Shamrock Foods · Operations Analytics Intern · Phoenix · 2026

Which fuel-card swipes deserve a second look?

The company had no process for monitoring fuel fraud in its fleet. I built the first one, from raw card transactions to a list an analyst can act on.

01Transactions2025 fleet fuel-card data
02JoinMatched to driver and shift records
03FeaturesOff-duty fueling, repeat fills, MPG, driver history
04ScorePCA reconstruction error per transaction
05ReportDaily detail for analysts, monthly summary for managers

What I decided

  • I tried Isolation Forest first and dropped it. It flagged transactions without saying why, and an analyst cannot question a driver over a score.
  • PCA reconstruction error shows which features made a transaction unusual, so every flag comes with a reason.
  • Two outputs for two readers: a daily table with full detail, and a monthly summary written in plain language.

What was hard

  • Card timestamps were in UTC and shift times were local. Until I mapped each site to its state time zone, ordinary on-shift fueling looked like off-duty fueling.

How I work

A flag needs a reason

If the person acting on a prediction cannot see why it was made, I pick a method that can show it.

Deliver a tool

The work is finished when someone else can use it without me: saved tables, a dashboard, an app.

Distrust the join

Most of my worst bugs were in how two tables met, like time zones. I check that before I tune anything.
